# Principles and thresholds

Four principles decide every change. Each one names its source so the proposal can cite it.

## 1. One axis per level

The main axis is product type (tops, bottoms, sets, jackets). Material, style, occasion and season are a second way to browse. Put them together under one top-level entry, for example "Shop by". Never put them beside the product types on the same level.

Attributes that describe a product (color, size, fabric, warehouse, shipping origin) belong in list-page filters, not in the menu.

Source: Baymard Institute, e-commerce navigation research 2025. 79 percent of sites turn filter attributes into categories.

## 2. Mobile first

Put product categories on the first level of the mobile menu. A shopper must reach any category page in two taps.

Source: Baymard 2025 lists "product categories are not top-level mobile navigation" as the first pitfall, found on 22 percent of sites. Measure the share of mobile traffic in the inputs and quote it in the proposal.

## 3. Data decides what stays

| Question | Data | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Can a page enter the menu | product count | If the page has fewer than 10 products, it gets no entry. |
| Is an entry worth its slot | Search Console clicks, GA4 views and key events | If all three are near zero over 12 months, remove the entry or mark it pending. |
| What is the label | search volume | The candidate with the larger US monthly volume wins. If the volumes are close, keep the site's own term. |
| How many items per level | count | About 10 is the limit. Above it, split into groups. |

Sources: Baymard 2025 (at least 10 products at the deepest level, about 10 items per level). Google Search Central, "Help Google understand your e-commerce site structure" (every product reachable through plain links from menu to category to product).

## 4. Menu only

Reuse existing pages. A need that requires a new, merged or deleted page, a redirect, a facet rule or a template change goes to the client-decisions section. The client owns those choices.

## Thresholds in one place

| Threshold | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum products for an entry | 10 | Baymard: the deepest level needs at least 10 products |
| Maximum items per level | about 10 | Baymard: users feel overwhelmed at ten or more options |
| Maximum top-level items | 10 | same source. Competitors range from 8 to 11 |
| Mobile depth | 2 taps to any category | Baymard pitfall 1 |
| Low-traffic removal | 12 months of data, views below a few thousand and key events near zero | observed in real stores. State the exact numbers in the proposal |
